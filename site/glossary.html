Glossary

Every word this handbook uses, defined like a friend would. Cross-links hop to the chapter where the word earned its definition.

All-in cost — Booth fee + table rental + parking + permits + lunch + everything. The number the five-question event test asks about, which is always bigger than the advertised fee.

Application — The form an organizer uses to pick vendors and plan the floor (power, tables, rows, jury). Matchmaking, mostly (applications).

Bring-formula — rate/hour × hours × crowd factor × 1.3 = what to pack. The one formula in the book you'll actually use weekly (restock math).

Booth fee — What you pay for the spot. Round-number shapes by event type live in chapter 1; the bargaining question is what's included (table? power? chair?).

Back-stock bin — The shelf under the table and the boxes in the van: stock that exists but is not yet displayed. The showroom is the table; the warehouse is the bin (restock math).

Buffer third (×1.3) — The multiplier on the bring-formula that absorbs the difference between last event and this one. Insurance, not greed.

Bungee — The stretchy cord that attaches everything (weights, totes, sidewalls, trash bags) to something portable. Might be the actual most-used tool at markets.

Cash dance (two-hop) — Say the total aloud, cash flat on the box, then count change into the customer's hand. The whole ritual, two hops, both out loud.

Cash float — The $85 of small bills and coins you lend the cash box at 10 a.m. so you can make change all day. Loaned, not earned — subtracted at close.

Chargeback — A card charge reversed by the customer's bank, at the seller's expense, weeks later. Rare at markets, real as weather. The defense is honest goods, honest totals said aloud, and a happy receipt trail (card readers).

COI (certificate of insurance) — The paper an organizer may ask for to prove your vendor liability policy exists. Ask an insurance professional — organizers tell you what they need; agents tell you what it costs (permits).

Crowd factor — In the bring-formula, how much bigger/smaller this event's traffic is than the one that taught you the rate. Same market: 1.0. "Double last year," believed: 1.5, at most.

Dead stock — Product nobody touched by close. A data point (pricing, placement, fit), not a guilt object (tracking).

DBA / trade name — A business display name different from the legal name. Listed in the "describe your work" slot; the legal name goes in the business-name slot (permits).

EIN — An Employer Identification Number: the IRS's free nine- digit ID for a business, so your personal number isn't on every form you hand an organizer (permits).

Event scorecard — The 1-5 verdict form you fill same-night, per event, that steers next season's bookings (scorecards).

Floor — The price of the three-layer cake below which you lose money per item: materials + labor + show share. Tags sit above the floor on purpose (pricing).

Hero / filler / experiment — The 70/25/5 stock split: your best line, its helpers, and the one new thing (restock math).

Jury — The screening some events do to pick a balanced room. Applies early, photos real, statement short (applications).

Load-in / load-out — The 90 minutes of setup before open and the 30-ish of teardown after. Two totes, one wagon, one order (booth setup, checklist).

LLC — Limited liability company; a common small-business structure. This book is LLC-flavored — it explains names and forms, and never advises on whether to form one (that's a professional conversation; see what this book is not).

Margin of the day — Net sales minus event costs: what the event actually added (tracking). The number that pays for upgrades.

Offline mode — The reader feature that queues card charges on your device for later push, when there's no signal. Understands its risk before the first gust day (card readers).

One-third rule — When a third of a line is gone, restock that line now. The visual math of a table that still looks alive.

Over/short — The recorded difference between what your sheet says you took and what the box says. An honest log line, not a scandal (tracking).

Permit / seller's permit — Formal state registration to sell taxable goods; the name varies by state. One phone call to your state's department of revenue settles most of it (permits).

Price raft — The three tiers (small/medium/large) that let a browser read your whole table in one glance (pricing).

Remit — Hand the collected tax money to the state, on their schedule. What happens after the envelope fills (permits).

Restock — Refilling a line mid-day from bin or van, before the table looks bare. Also the math chapter that decides what you pack (restock math).

RV / GSE note — Vehicles and generators at events have their own organizer rules; ask before you assume anything about parking, power, or idling (questions).

Sales tax — The buyer-paid portion, collected by you if your state requires it, remitted on their schedule. Recorded as a set-aside, not an opinion (tracking).

Sell-through rate — Units sold ÷ hours open. The rate that feeds the bring-formula. Slow hours counted separately, for festivals' sake (restock math).

Sidewall — Zip-on canopy walls for windward weather; one or two is plenty. The difference between "outdoor booth" and "outdoor booth in spring rules" (weather).

SKU — Stock-keeping unit: one product-line/variant combination on your restock list. Pencil-level bookkeeping, not software (restock math).

Switch scam — The practiced "wait, I gave you bigger bills" claim at busy tills. The cash dance is the whole vaccine.

Tally sheet — The seven-column per-event record of every sale: item, qty, price, cash, card, notes, and the totals footer (tracking).

Vendor contract — The agreement behind "I paid for a spot": fee, refund-on-rain rule, load-in times, bump rules. Read before you pay (permits).

Waitlist — The queue behind a full event. Not rejection — a chance at a moving slot. Answer waitlist offers within a day or two (finding events).

W-9 — The organizer's vendor-ID form: name, address, TIN. A form, not a payment (permits).

Wet go/no-go rule — Your pre-written stop conditions for storm-day calls, filled out in sunny weather (scorecards, weather).

The Side-Hustle Reseller Handbook — The companion book: selling online (eBay/Amazon), listings, shipping, marketplace fees. This book's in-person counterpart, published by the same folks (relationship).
