# Start Here

Welcome. Maybe you have a garage shelf of hand-made candles, a spreadsheet
finally sorted, and a browser tab permanently open that says *"how much do
booths cost?"* This handbook was written for you — with zero judgment
attached. It's about **selling in person**: craft fairs, farmers markets,
vendor shows, and conventions — a real small-business move (Lots of people
run it under an LLC; plenty of people don't, yet) that this book explains
the way a friend would: plainly, step by step, never condescending.

It's the in-person companion to **The Side-Hustle Reseller Handbook**
(selling online on eBay/Amazon). Different venue, same temperament: calm,
honest, no get-rich-quick anywhere in the room.

## The promise of this book

By the end, you'll be able to answer these questions in your own words:

1. **Which events are worth my money and my Saturday?** (A five-question
   filter, and a free way to scout before you spend.)
2. **What's on an application, and what is a sales-tax permit really
   asking?** (Plain English, no fear, one phone call that solves most of
   it.)
3. **What do I actually need for a booth?** (A plan that starts around
   $150 and skips exactly none of the things that make people say "ooh,
   nice booth.")
4. **What should things cost and where does the change come from?**
   (Round prices, a $85 starting float, and the two-hop cash dance.)
5. **How do card readers work and how do I not look silly the first
   time?** (A dry-run ritual you do at your kitchen table.)
6. **What do I write down, honestly, so the numbers teach me something?**
   (One tally sheet, reconciled to the penny by 10 p.m.)
7. **How much stock should I bring?** (Restock math with a hero/filler/
   experiment split.)
8. **What do I do when the day is slow or the sky turns mean?** (Ten
   moves for slow hours, a canopies-to-tents wet plan, and taking care
   of the seller — you.)
9. **And after it all — what do I actually review?** (A 30-minute ritual
   that decides next season, on purpose.)

You will not need a warehouse, a niche emporium, a 40-page business plan,
or a single borrowed dollar for any of it.

## The ground rules we wrote by

Three promises, kept on every page:

1. **No condescension.** If a word needs defining, we define it the first
   time (see the [Glossary](glossary.md)). If a step sounds obvious, we
   say it anyway — because "obvious" is only obvious once you know it.
2. **Round numbers, clearly flagged.** Booth fees, card-reader rates,
   and float figures here are rounded on purpose so you can do them in
   your head. When a number is a guess, we say so.
3. **Low stakes first.** Your first event is a test, not a verdict. We
   will send you to a one-day, low-fee market before we ever send you
   to a two-day convention.

## Selling in person vs. the reseller book

**The Side-Hustle Reseller Handbook** covers selling *online*: listings,
photos, shipping labels, marketplace fees, and quitting politely. This
book covers selling *in person*: the room you can't retake, the change
you must make, and the weather you can't reschedule. The books share the
same bones — one simple records table, scam-radar, and the "quit or
change course before you sour" instinct — and cross-pollinate freely:

- The reseller book's **sold-price research** is this book's event
  research: look at what actually happened, not what was hoped.
- The reseller book's **TAXES envelope** habit works just as well after
  market days (see [Honest sales tracking](06-honest-sales-tracking.md)).
- When events leave you holding leftover stock online-first, the reseller
  book's lot-bundling ideas are the polite cleanup crew.

Neither book requires the other to be useful.

## What this handbook is not

- It is not tax, legal, or financial advice. "LLC-flavored" means the
  book assumes a small formal business *might* exist, and explains names,
  permits, and forms in plain language — it never tells you what structure
  to choose or what to owe. When a decision gets real (money, legal,
  health), a real professional belongs in the conversation.
- It is not a get-rich scheme, and it will not promise that foot traffic
  goes up. Markets are a real job with real hours — standing in wind,
  even. We just want the hours to be calm.
- It is not finished. Event norms and payment-app policies change; this
  book is versioned, updated, and open source under the MIT license.
  Corrections welcome.

## The one-paragraph version of the whole book

Pick an event whose *shoppers* match your *stuff* — scout it in person
first with the [event scorecard](event-scorecards.md) before you pay any
booth fee. Apply early; ask the organizer what paperwork they require and
[what the state asks from you](02-applications-and-permits.md) before you
pay, not after. Build a booth that reads at ten feet and packs into two
totes ([booth on a budget](03-booth-setup-on-a-budget.md)). Price in
whole dollars, start with an $85 float, and make change with the two-hop
dance ([pricing](04-pricing-and-making-change.md)). Take cards with a
reader you rehearsed at your kitchen table, and know your offline plan
([card readers](05-card-readers.md)). Track every sale in one honest
sheet and count the box at close ([tracking](06-honest-sales-tracking.md)).
Bring stock by the hero/filler/experiment split, restock before you sell
out ([restock math](07-restock-math.md)), and survive slow hours and ugly
skies with a plan, not a shrug ([slow days & weather](08-slow-days-and-weather.md)).
Then do the 30-minute review the same night and let it decide your next
date — not your mood ([after-event review](09-after-event-review.md)).
