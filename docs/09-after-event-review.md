# The After-Event Review Ritual

The day is done, the boxes are in the hall, your feet are loud. This
chapter is fifteen to thirty minutes — tonight, not next week — that
converts one event's chaos into next season's decisions. It's the
shortest chapter in the book and the one that compounds hardest.

## Why tonight (and not "I'll write it this weekend")

Memory decays *specifically*: 48 hours from now you will remember
the sales total and forget the wave of three people who asked for
blue, the guy at 11 a.m. who said your table was hard to see past,
and the fact that the reader app's offline mode queued silently
all afternoon. Those specifics are where the value is; the ritual is
a ransom for them, paid promptly.

## The 30-minute ritual, in order

Run it at the kitchen table with the day's paper in front of you:

1. **Close the money loop.** Finish the
   [end-of-day count](04-pricing-and-making-change.md) and the
   [tally sheet totals](06-honest-sales-tracking.md) — cash, card,
   gross, refunds, costs, margin. The day is not over until the
   sheet's footer is filled.
2. **Fill the scorecard.** The [event
   scorecard](event-scorecards.md) — traffic, fit, costs, neighbors,
   weather luck, organizer quality, the 1-5 verdict. Five minutes,
   at most; the numbers are still warm.
3. **Answer the five review questions** (one line each, written):
   - *What was my goal going in, and did I hit it?* (Money? Contacts?
     Photos? A new-line test? Set in [chapter 1](01-finding-events.md).)
   - **Best seller — and why it was** (placement? price raft? the
     demo?)
   - **Worst seller — and what that means** (price? placement? a
     [dead stock](glossary.md) verdict?)
   - **What did people ask for that I didn't have?** (This is the
     season's actual product roadmap, one line at a time.)
   - **What one change would improve the day by the most?** (One
     only. Ten ideas is a wish list; one is a plan.)
4. **Write the three lists.** *Keep* (what worked — specific
   arrangements, prices, phrases), *change* (one thing, rolled
   forward), *stop* (what you'll leave in the van from now on).
5. **Backup the paper.** Photo the tally sheet and scorecard into
   your events folder; the
   [weekly roll-up](06-honest-sales-tracking.md) gets its line.
6. **Thank the organizer.** One short message — "Thanks, good show,
   I'll be in touch for the next one" (or the honest version). This
   is 20% kindness, and it's genuinely useful next time you need a
   waitlist favor, a re-seating, or a reference.
7. **Send the restock note to yourself.** The make/buy list from
   [restock math](07-restock-math.md) — anything under "truly
   available = 0" that you'll want for a future booking, now dated
   so it doesn't ambush you the week before the next event.

That's the whole thing. Thirty minutes, pencil, done.

## The five questions in their natural habitat

For the full-season view, those five answers stack neatly in one
table — the honest aftermath of a year:

| Question | Farmers market, wk 6 | Spring fair day 1 | Holiday market 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Goal hit? | steady day (Y/N) | photos + margin (Y) | mostly (N) |
| Best seller | candles @ $15 | ornament bundle | gift set |
| Why | demo visibility | first-in aisle | price raft |
| Worst | soap singles | wood squares | candle minis |
| Asked-for | blue! (3x) | lavender (2x) | larger size |
| One change | riser higher | more quilted case markup | bundle tags printed |

Stack a season of rows and the *bookings question answers itself*
— which is the point of filling in the columns *tonight* instead of
season's end, with whole rows of faint memory.

## Rebooking (decide before the invoice arrives)

Organizers often open next-year's registrations *fast* — sometimes
the same month. You don't have to answer fast; you have to answer
*honestly*, and the honest input is on your scorecard and roll-up:

- **Return as-is** — 4-5 scorecard, margin positive, and the day's
  costs were on plan.
- **Return with one change** — scorecard good, margin wobbly (the
  change list says what to change; often it's stock split or
  table layout, both cheap to fix).
- **Pass this year, follow the event's history** — low scorecard,
  low margin, sad organizer community chat. Politely rebook *next*
  year only if the event itself changes shape (new location, new
  management, bigger marketing).
- **Never rebook out of guilt or momentum.** The same [review
  honesty](06-honest-sales-tracking.md) that steered restock
  steers this decision.

Booking reply etiquette, briefly: if you *won't* return, say so
politely within a week (their planning costs them real months); if
you *will*, they'll thank you quickly and you'll be on the
returning-vendor list ahead of the public rush.

## The season-level review (once a year)

One evening a year, before the next season's bookings — the annual
parent-teacher conference with your own small business:

1. **Cost per $100 of sales, per event.** (Event cost ÷ gross ×
   100.) A booth costing $115 that grossed $400 produces "$29 per
   $100" — round number, instantly comparable across events.
2. **Margin by product line**, across the whole year. Pays off the
   [three-price raft](04-pricing-and-making-change.md) decisions
   from the season.
3. **Margin by event type** — markets vs fairs vs cons, and decide
   where next year's *weekends* go. Most sellers find one type
   quietly paying for everything else; most years the losing type
   deserves either a fix or a farewell.
4. **The fun check.** The reseller handbook's quit-chapter asks
   "is it still fun?" and this book inherits the question
   unchanged. Some years the honest answer is "not with these
   events" (change the events); occasionally it's "not this
   business shape" — and the honest-management answer to that is
   the same calm wind-down the reseller book describes: sell
   through, bundle the leftovers (the reseller book's
   lot-bundling ideas apply), and put the booth kit in storage
   with a date on it.

## Photo archive + receipts

Two two-minute closes for the ritual:

- **Photos of the booth as-set** — one event, every event, into the
  same `booth-photos` folder used for
  [applications](02-applications-and-permits.md). Next January you'll
  see how far the display traveled.
- **Receipts and permit paperwork** into the paperwork kit's folder
  at home: booth-fee receipt, parking receipts, materials receipts
  (they were business expenses if you bought them for this), and
  your sales-tax filings from the
  [permits chapter](02-applications-and-permits.md). This folder is
  what makes (a) your next year's permit filings dull, and (b) the
  accountant conversation — if you have one — short and cheap.

## The ritual on days you don't want to do it

Some days end in rain, zero refunds, or a checked-out crowd, and
the ritual feels like homework nobody assigned. Do the two-minute
version instead of the thirty:

- Fill in the scorecard numbers only.
- Write one line in the five-questions slot: goal hit? Y/N.
- One sentence verdict: "Worth it? [Y/N/Maybe], one change: ___."
- Photos of the paper into the folder.

Two minutes today beats nine lines next Saturday, when you'll have
to re-derive from scratch the fact that the wave came at 1:40 p.m.
(the [slow-hours note](08-slow-days-and-weather.md) will remind you
why that matters for *next* year's event pick).
